    Geometry

    Coordinate system: the wall is the plane z = 0. The box sits on the wall and extends outward in +z. The box occupies x ∈ [-W/2, W/2], y ∈ [-H/2, H/2], z ∈ [0, d]. The back of the box is sealed against the wall; light reaches the wall only through cutouts in the four side walls.

    Light source at L = (0, 0, cz), where cz is the bulb's height along the depth axis. The "Light z" slider sets cz = lightFrac · d; 50% places the bulb at the geometric center (d/2). Lower values pull the bulb toward the wall (more of the box becomes "in front of" the light and is unusable for cutouts); higher values push it forward. Lid is parallel to the wall at z = cz + h, blocking forward glare.

    Forward map

    For a cutout at (u, z) on the right wall (x = W/2), the ray from L through that point hits the wall at:

    t      = c_z / (c_z - z)
X_wall = (W/2) · t
Y_wall = u · t

    Symmetrically for the other three walls (swap roles of x and y for top/bottom; flip sign for left/bot). Valid only when z < cz — cutouts past the light project forward, not toward the wall.

    Inverse map

    For a target wall point (X, Y) outside the box footprint, decide which side wall handles it by comparing |X| - W/2 vs |Y| - H/2 — whichever is larger picks the dominant face. Then back-solve:

    // for the right face (X > 0, |X|-W/2 >= |Y|-H/2)
t      = X / (W/2)
z_cut  = c_z · (1 - 1/t)
u_cut  = Y / t

    Constraints: 0 < z_cut < cz - margin, |u_cut| < H/2. Points violating these are unreachable on this wall.

    Why the maximum lit region is roughly a circle

    The point source plus rotational symmetry of the box-around-light means that, at equal cutout-z values, equal-radius wall points are reachable. Practically the achievable radius is limited by:

    • Structural margin near z = d/2 — you can't carve infinitely close to the light's z-level.
    • Inverse-square brightness falloff — far points become invisibly dim.
    • Penumbra blur — finite bulb size produces soft shadows, blurrier the closer the cutout is to the bulb (or the farther from the wall).

    Penumbra estimate

    For a bulb of diameter D, the blur radius on the wall at a point reached via a cutout at depth z_cut:

    blur ≈ D · (z_cut to wall distance) / (bulb to cutout distance)
     = D · z_cut / (c_z - z_cut)

    So as z_cut → cz, blur diverges. Substituting z_cut via the inverse map gives blur = D · (2X/W − 1) for the right face — independent of cz. The light-z slider therefore doesn't change geometric image sharpness; it changes how much of the side panel is usable (more depth on the wall side) and where the cutouts physically sit.

    Brightness model

    Wall illuminance at point (X, Y) reached through a cutout of area A:

    E ≈ I · A · cos(θ) / r²

    where r is the source-to-wall distance, θ is the angle between the ray and the wall normal. To make outer regions match inner brightness, scale cutout area by roughly r² / cos(θ).
